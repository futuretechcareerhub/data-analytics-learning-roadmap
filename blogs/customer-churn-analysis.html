Customer Churn Analysis: How to Find Why Customers Leave Using SQL & Power BI

A practical guide to measuring churn, understanding customer behavior and turning retention data into business actions.

Published: September 26, 2026

Quick answer: Customer churn analysis identifies which customers stop using or paying for a product or service, when they leave, and which patterns are associated with their departure. Analysts define churn clearly, calculate it for a consistent customer cohort and time period, segment the results, investigate possible drivers, and communicate evidence-backed actions to improve retention.

Churn analysis does not automatically prove why a customer left. Billing data, usage patterns and support interactions can reveal associations and useful hypotheses, but causal claims usually require additional investigation or experiments.

What Is Customer Churn?

Customer churn occurs when a customer stops being an active customer under a business's agreed definition. In a subscription business, churn may mean a paid subscription is canceled or expires. In retail, a customer who has not purchased recently may be considered inactive only after a defined period, because there is not always an explicit cancellation event.

Before calculating churn, confirm the business model, event date and customer status rules with stakeholders. A definition that works for monthly subscriptions may not work for a grocery retailer with naturally irregular purchase intervals.

Customer Churn Rate Formula

A common customer churn rate for a period is:

Customer churn rate = Customers lost during the period ÷ Customers at the start of the period × 100

For example, if a subscription business starts a month with 2,000 customers and 80 of those starting customers churn during the month, the simple period churn rate is 80 ÷ 2,000 × 100 = 4%.

State exactly how you handle new customers acquired during the period, reactivations, paused accounts, duplicate accounts and customers who change plans. The denominator and event rules should remain consistent when comparing periods.

Data Needed for a Churn Analysis

A practical subscription dataset may include customers, subscription events, invoices, product usage and support interactions. Common fields are:

  • Customer or account ID (stable and consistently defined).
  • Signup date, subscription start date and cancellation or expiration date.
  • Subscription status, plan, billing frequency and recurring revenue.
  • Usage events, such as active days or key feature adoption, aggregated to an appropriate level.
  • Support ticket dates, categories and resolution times, subject to access and privacy controls.
  • Acquisition channel, customer segment and relevant product or region attributes.

Use only data that is appropriate for the analysis and permitted by your organization. Avoid placing direct identifiers or sensitive customer details in public portfolio projects; use anonymized or synthetic data.

SQL: Calculate Monthly Churn from Subscription Data

Assume a simplified table named subscriptions with one row per customer subscription and columns customer_id, start_date, cancel_date. This example counts customers active at the beginning of a calendar month and among those, customers whose cancellation date falls within that month. Adapt the logic for your data model, time zone, reactivations and status rules.

-- Example syntax uses PostgreSQL date functions.
WITH months AS (
  SELECT generate_series(
    DATE '2026-01-01',
    DATE '2026-06-01',
    INTERVAL '1 month'
  )::date AS month_start
),
starting_customers AS (
  SELECT
    m.month_start,
    s.customer_id,
    s.cancel_date
  FROM months m
  JOIN subscriptions s
    ON s.start_date < m.month_start
   AND (s.cancel_date IS NULL OR s.cancel_date >= m.month_start)
),
monthly_counts AS (
  SELECT
    month_start,
    COUNT(DISTINCT customer_id) AS customers_at_start,
    COUNT(DISTINCT customer_id) FILTER (
      WHERE cancel_date >= month_start
        AND cancel_date < month_start + INTERVAL '1 month'
    ) AS customers_lost
  FROM starting_customers
  GROUP BY month_start
)
SELECT
  month_start,
  customers_at_start,
  customers_lost,
  ROUND(
    100.0 * customers_lost
    / NULLIF(customers_at_start, 0), 2
  ) AS churn_rate_pct
FROM monthly_counts
ORDER BY month_start;

This is a teaching example, not a universal subscription-accounting definition. In some systems, a cancellation request occurs before the paid service actually ends. Decide whether churn is measured at request date, service end date or another contractual event. If one customer can have multiple subscriptions, decide whether the unit of analysis is the customer, account or subscription.

Analyze Which Customer Segments Have Higher Churn

After validating the overall measure, break it down into business-relevant groups. Examples include:

  • Customer tenure: newly acquired versus long-standing customers.
  • Plan or product: monthly versus annual plans, product tier or feature package.
  • Acquisition channel or campaign, when attribution is reliable.
  • Region, store, business size or customer type, where permitted and meaningful.
  • Usage bands: customers who adopted a key feature versus those who did not.
  • Support experience: ticket categories or resolution time, with careful interpretation.

Always show both churn rate and the number of customers in a segment. A very high percentage based on a tiny group may be less stable than a moderate rate across a large group. Check whether segment definitions changed over time.

Turn Analysis into Business Recommendations

Translate observed patterns into testable actions. For example, if new customers show lower early retention than older cohorts, examine onboarding completion, time-to-first-value, product usability and acquisition mix. If churn is associated with unresolved support issues, review ticket categories and resolution workflows.

Separate what the data shows from what you infer. A segment may have higher churn and lower usage, but that alone does not establish that low usage caused churn. Validate hypotheses with customer research, controlled experiments or additional analysis where feasible.

Recommendations should include an owner, target segment, proposed action, success metric and review period. A retention campaign can be evaluated using an appropriate control group when practical, while monitoring unintended effects such as discount dependency or customer complaints.

Common Mistakes in Churn Analysis

  • Using inconsistent churn definitions across reports.
  • Mixing customers and subscriptions as the unit of analysis.
  • Ignoring new customers added after the period begins when defining the denominator.
  • Counting cancellation requests as churn without checking when service ends.
  • Comparing cohorts with different observation windows.
  • Reporting only percentages without customer counts or uncertainty.
  • Calling correlation a root cause or promising a retention improvement without evidence.
  • Exposing personal or confidential customer information in dashboards or portfolio repositories.

Portfolio Project: Subscription Churn Analysis

Build a portfolio project with public, licensed or synthetic data. Include:

  1. Business objective and an explicit definition of customer churn.
  2. Data dictionary and documented data quality checks.
  3. SQL queries for monthly churn and customer segments.
  4. Retention or cohort analysis with comparable observation windows.
  5. Power BI report with metric definitions, date filters and counts.
  6. Three to five evidence-backed findings, each with a caveat or limitation.
  7. Practical recommendations and a plan to test them.
  8. A README explaining assumptions, data sources and how to reproduce the work.

Do not claim real-world revenue saved or churn reduction unless you measured it with a credible method. Label simulated data and hypothetical recommendations clearly.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good customer churn rate?

There is no single good rate for every business. Churn varies by industry, customer type, contract length, product and measurement window. Compare with the organization's own historical performance and relevant, trustworthy benchmarks using equivalent definitions.

What is the difference between churn and retention?

Churn measures customers lost under a defined rule; retention measures customers who remain. For a fixed starting cohort and consistent period, the two may complement each other, but acquisition, reactivation and denominator choices affect the relationship.

Can SQL be used for customer churn analysis?

Yes. SQL is useful for defining customer populations, calculating period metrics, building cohorts, joining subscription and usage data, and segmenting churn. Validate date logic and customer-level definitions carefully.

How is churn analysis different from churn prediction?

Churn analysis describes historical patterns and investigates potential drivers. Churn prediction uses features available at a prediction time to estimate which customers may churn later. Predictive models require time-aware validation and careful monitoring.

Which Power BI visuals are useful for churn?

Trend lines, KPI cards, cohort matrices, segment bar charts and customer-count tables can help. Choose visuals based on stakeholder questions and display metric definitions and data coverage.

Does high churn in a segment prove the cause?

No. It identifies an association or a group to investigate. Causal conclusions need stronger evidence, such as suitable experiments, research or a credible causal analysis design.
