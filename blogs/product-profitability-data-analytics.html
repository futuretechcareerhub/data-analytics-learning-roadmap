Why Are Some Products Making More Money Than Others? A Data Analytics Investigation of Product Profitability

A product can lead the sales chart and still be a weak contributor to profit. Analysts need to look beyond revenue, understand costs and discounts, and find the products that genuinely improve a business's bottom line.

Published: November 21, 2026 · Excel · SQL · Power BI

1. The business question: revenue or profitability?

Imagine an online retailer reviewing its monthly product report. A few products generate impressive sales, but finance leaders are unsure whether those products are worth the discounts, shipping expenses and operating costs attached to them. The team needs a product-level view of profit—not just sales volume.

Questions to answer:

  • Which products generate the most revenue and gross profit?
  • Which products have strong sales but thin margins?
  • Are discounts helping volume enough to offset margin reduction?
  • Do patterns differ by category, region or sales channel?
  • Where should the business test pricing, promotion or assortment changes?
Note: Example numbers below are illustrative, not actual company results.

2. Clean and validate the dataset in Excel

Use transaction-level columns such as Order ID, Date, Product, Category, Quantity, Unit Price, Unit Cost, Discount, Shipping Cost and Region. Before calculating profit, remove verified duplicate lines, standardize labels, validate dates and numeric fields, flag missing costs, check discount formats and reconcile revenue against source totals.

For discounts stored as percentages, define these metrics clearly:

Net Revenue = Quantity × Unit Price × (1 − Discount %)
Gross Profit = Net Revenue − (Quantity × Unit Cost)
Gross Margin % = Gross Profit ÷ Net Revenue

Shipping can be included when the business has a dependable allocation rule. Do not call the result net profit unless all relevant costs are included.

3. Use SQL for product-level performance

Assume a hypothetical sales table with discount as a decimal (0.10 = 10%). Aggregate the measures and compare both profit and margin:

SELECT product_name,
  SUM(quantity) AS units_sold,
  SUM(quantity * unit_price * (1 - discount)) AS net_revenue,
  SUM(quantity * unit_price * (1 - discount)
      - quantity * unit_cost) AS gross_profit,
  ROUND(100.0 * SUM(quantity * unit_price * (1 - discount)
      - quantity * unit_cost)
      / NULLIF(SUM(quantity * unit_price * (1 - discount)), 0), 2)
      AS gross_margin_pct
FROM sales
GROUP BY product_name
ORDER BY gross_profit DESC;

Sort by gross profit to understand total contribution, then separately sort by margin. A high margin on a small sales base may contribute little money; a lower-margin item can still create substantial profit.

5. Design a Power BI profitability dashboard

  • KPI cards: net revenue, gross profit, gross margin and units sold.
  • Scatter plot: revenue vs. margin, with bubble size representing profit or volume.
  • Product ranking: top and bottom products by gross profit.
  • Discount analysis: compare discount bands against margin and units.
  • Trend chart: monthly profit and margin over time.
  • Filters: category, region, channel, month and product.
Dashboard validation: Check totals against the source, confirm discount logic, test slicers, and document the margin definition.

6. Investigate possible drivers

  • Discount depth: compare discount levels with volume and margin; test causality carefully.
  • Cost changes: review supplier costs, packaging, shipping or fulfillment.
  • Product mix: find whether a category's result is concentrated in a few items.
  • Channel and region: include fees or delivery costs when comparable.
  • Returns: check whether return and cancellation effects are captured.

8. What this case study demonstrates

It combines spreadsheet validation, SQL aggregation, metric definitions, visualization and business communication. A strong portfolio project documents the business question, assumptions, data cleaning, query, dashboard, insights, action plan and limitations—such as whether overhead, shipping and returns were included.
