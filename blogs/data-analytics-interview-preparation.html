SQL Interview Preparation

SQL is often tested through practical questions rather than isolated syntax. The most useful preparation is to solve problems that resemble business analysis.

Core SQL topics

  • SELECT, WHERE and ORDER BY
  • GROUP BY, HAVING and aggregate functions
  • INNER, LEFT and other common JOIN patterns
  • CASE expressions
  • Subqueries
  • Common table expressions (CTEs)
  • Window functions such as ROW_NUMBER, RANK and running totals
  • Date and time calculations
  • NULL handling and duplicate records

How to approach a SQL interview problem

  1. Clarify the required output and business definition.
  2. Identify the tables and relationships you need.
  3. Check the level of detail, or grain, of each table.
  4. Filter the relevant records.
  5. Join only the tables required for the question.
  6. Aggregate at the correct level.
  7. Use CTEs or window functions when they make the logic clearer.
  8. Check edge cases such as NULLs, duplicates and ties.

Example SQL question

Question: Find the top three products by monthly revenue and show the month-over-month change.

Preparation focus: Be ready to discuss date grouping, revenue calculation, aggregation, ranking, window functions and how you would handle ties or missing months. The interviewer may care as much about your reasoning as the final query.

Excel Interview Preparation

Excel questions can test both feature knowledge and practical problem solving. Prepare to work with a dataset rather than simply define functions.

Useful Excel areas

  • IF and logical formulas
  • XLOOKUP or other lookup approaches
  • SUMIFS, COUNTIFS and conditional calculations
  • Text and date functions
  • Sorting, filtering and data validation
  • Removing duplicates and handling inconsistent values
  • PivotTables and PivotCharts
  • Basic charts and summary reporting

When solving an Excel task, explain what you are trying to calculate and why you selected the method. If several functions can solve the problem, focus on correctness, readability and maintainability.

Power BI and Dashboard Interview Preparation

Dashboard interviews may ask you to interpret an existing report, explain a design choice or describe how you would build a report from raw data.

Topics to review

  • Data import and transformation
  • Power Query concepts
  • Relationships and basic data modeling
  • Measures versus calculated columns
  • DAX fundamentals
  • Filters, slicers and drill-down
  • KPI design
  • Chart selection and dashboard hierarchy
  • Data storytelling and readability

How to discuss a dashboard

Start with the audience and decision. Then explain which KPIs matter, how the data was prepared, what patterns are visible, and what further investigation is required. Avoid treating every visible trend as a business conclusion without checking the underlying data.

Statistics and Analytical Reasoning

Data analyst interviews may include statistics because analysts need to interpret variation and relationships correctly. You do not need to turn every interview into a mathematics exercise; focus on understanding and explaining the concepts.

  • Mean, median and mode
  • Range, variance and standard deviation
  • Percentiles and distributions
  • Correlation and its limitations
  • Sampling and selection bias
  • Confidence intervals at a conceptual level
  • Hypothesis testing fundamentals
  • Basic regression interpretation
Interview habit: When giving a statistical answer, explain what the result means in the business context and mention relevant assumptions or limitations. A numerical result without interpretation is often incomplete.

Data Analyst Case Study Preparation

A case study tests how you structure an ambiguous problem. There may not be one perfect answer, so make your assumptions explicit and show the path from question to evidence.

A practical case-study framework

  1. Clarify the objective: What decision or outcome is the business trying to understand?
  2. Define the metric: Decide how success or change will be measured.
  3. Identify relevant data: List the fields, tables or sources you would need.
  4. Check data quality: Consider missing values, duplicates, definitions and time periods.
  5. Segment the problem: Break results down by useful dimensions such as product, region, customer group or channel.
  6. Compare periods or groups: Look for meaningful differences and trends.
  7. Form hypotheses: Suggest plausible explanations that can be tested against the data.
  8. State limitations: Separate evidence from assumptions and note what the data cannot establish.
  9. Communicate the next step: Explain what should be investigated, monitored or tested next.

Example case

Scenario: An e-commerce business reports a decline in completed orders.

A structured response would check whether the decline is real, define the reporting period, inspect traffic and conversion metrics, compare device and channel segments, investigate funnel stages, check changes in product availability or returns, and identify which evidence supports further investigation. Do not assume a cause before checking the data.

Project Discussion Preparation

Your projects can become some of the most useful interview evidence because they give you a real example of how you work. Prepare to discuss the project without relying on a memorized script.

Use this project walkthrough

  1. What was the business or analytical problem?
  2. Who was the intended audience?
  3. Where did the data come from?
  4. What data-quality issues did you find?
  5. Which tools did you use and why?
  6. How did you define the important KPIs?
  7. What analysis did you perform?
  8. What was the most important finding?
  9. What assumptions or limitations affected the analysis?
  10. What would you improve or investigate next?

Review your portfolio projects from the perspective of an interviewer. If you cannot explain a calculation, join, dashboard metric or analytical decision you included, revisit that part before the interview.

Data Analyst Interview Questions to Practise

SQL

  • How would you find duplicate records in a table?
  • What is the difference between WHERE and HAVING?
  • When would you use a LEFT JOIN?
  • How would you find the second-highest value in a group?
  • When would a window function be useful?

Excel

  • When would you use a PivotTable?
  • How would you identify and clean duplicate records?
  • How would you compare two datasets in Excel?
  • Which lookup approach would you choose and why?

Power BI and dashboards

  • What makes a dashboard useful for a business audience?
  • What is the difference between a measure and a calculated column?
  • How would you decide which KPIs belong on the first page?
  • How would you validate a dashboard before sharing it?

Statistics and analysis

  • When can the median be more informative than the mean?
  • What does standard deviation tell you?
  • What is correlation, and why does it not automatically prove causation?
  • How would you investigate an unexpected change in a KPI?

Behavioral and communication questions

  • Tell me about a project where the data was messy.
  • Describe a time you had to explain a technical result to a non-technical person.
  • How do you handle a request when the business question is unclear?
  • What do you do when your analysis contradicts an initial assumption?

How to Answer Data Analyst Interview Questions Clearly

A strong answer can be structured as context → approach → result → limitation or next step. For technical questions, explain the logic before or alongside the syntax. For case studies, state assumptions and show how you would validate them.

For example, if asked why a KPI fell, do not immediately name a cause. Start by confirming the KPI definition and period, validate the data, segment the metric, compare relevant dimensions, and then discuss hypotheses supported by evidence.

Common Data Analyst Interview Preparation Mistakes

  • Memorizing question banks: practise unfamiliar variations so you learn the underlying concepts.
  • Ignoring business context: a technically correct query can still answer the wrong question.
  • Weak SQL fundamentals: do not jump to advanced syntax before mastering joins and aggregation.
  • Dashboard-only preparation: review data cleaning, KPI definitions and analytical reasoning too.
  • Overclaiming project results: distinguish observations from assumptions and causal claims.
  • Unable to explain your own portfolio: revisit every important calculation and design decision.
  • No timed practice: practise solving questions within realistic time limits.
  • Long, unfocused answers: lead with the key point and then explain the reasoning.

Frequently Asked Questions About Data Analyst Interview Preparation

How should I start Data Analyst Interview Preparation?

Start by assessing SQL, Excel, visualization, statistics, case-study and project skills. Then build a practice schedule that combines concept review with timed problems.

What SQL topics should a data analyst prepare for interviews?

Prepare filtering, aggregation, joins, CASE expressions, subqueries, CTEs, window functions, date calculations, NULL handling and duplicate detection.

Are SQL questions difficult for data analyst interviews?

Difficulty varies by role. Practise both fundamentals and business-style problems so you can adapt to unfamiliar questions rather than relying only on memorized queries.

What Excel questions are asked in data analyst interviews?

Common areas include formulas, lookups, conditional calculations, PivotTables, cleaning, sorting, filtering and practical data summaries.

How should I prepare for Power BI interview questions?

Review data transformation, relationships, measures, DAX fundamentals, filters, KPI design, visualization choices and dashboard validation. Be prepared to explain why you designed a report in a particular way.

What statistics should a data analyst know for interviews?

Focus on descriptive statistics, distributions, variability, percentiles, correlation, sampling and basic hypothesis-testing concepts, with an emphasis on interpretation.

How do I prepare for a Data Analyst Case Study?

Practise clarifying the objective, defining metrics, identifying data, checking quality, segmenting results, testing hypotheses, stating limitations and communicating next steps.

How should I explain my data analytics projects in an interview?

Explain the problem, audience, data source, cleaning work, tools, KPIs, analysis, findings, limitations and next steps. Be ready to defend important technical decisions.

How long should I prepare for a data analyst interview?

There is no universal duration. A focused plan can be organized around skill gaps, available practice time and the complexity of the role.

Can a data analytics course help with interview preparation?

A structured course can help organize learning and provide project practice, but interview readiness also requires independent problem solving, timed practice and the ability to explain your own work.

Final Takeaway

Effective Data Analyst Interview Preparation is a practice system, not a memorization exercise. Build SQL fluency, strengthen Excel and dashboard skills, understand practical statistics, practise case studies, and prepare to explain your projects with evidence and limitations.

The most useful final test is simple: can you take an unfamiliar analytical question, clarify it, work through the data logically, explain your result and communicate what should happen next?
