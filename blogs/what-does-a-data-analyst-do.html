What Does a Data Analyst Do? Roles, Skills, Tools & Real-World Workflow

A practical guide to what data analysts actually do, the tools they use, and how analysis turns business data into useful decisions.

What does a data analyst do? A data analyst examines data to answer business questions, identify patterns, measure performance and communicate useful findings. The work is not simply about making charts. It usually involves understanding a business problem, preparing reliable data, analysing it, visualising the results and explaining what the findings mean.

For beginners, understanding this workflow is useful because it shows which skills matter in real analyst work and how tools such as Excel, SQL, Power BI and Python fit together.

What Does a Data Analyst Actually Do?

A data analyst works between raw data and business decisions. A typical assignment might involve questions such as: Which products are underperforming? Why did customer conversions change? Which region is growing fastest? Where are operational delays occurring?

The analyst translates these questions into measurable requirements, finds and prepares the relevant data, performs analysis and presents evidence that stakeholders can use. The exact responsibilities vary by company, but the central goal is to turn data into actionable information.

A Data Analyst's Real-World Workflow

  1. Understand the business question: Clarify the decision, metric or problem that needs to be investigated.
  2. Collect and access data: Identify suitable databases, spreadsheets, reports or other approved data sources.
  3. Clean and prepare the data: Handle missing values, inconsistent formats, duplicates and other quality issues.
  4. Explore and analyse: Use queries, calculations and statistical reasoning to identify trends, relationships and unusual results.
  5. Build visualisations: Select charts, dashboards or reports that make important patterns easier to understand.
  6. Interpret the findings: Connect the analysis back to the original business question rather than stopping at the numbers.
  7. Communicate recommendations: Explain the evidence, limitations and possible actions to stakeholders.
Key idea: A strong analyst does not only answer “what happened?” The analysis should help stakeholders understand “why it happened?” and, where the data supports it, “what should we investigate or do next?”

Core Skills Every Data Analyst Needs

  • Excel: Useful for calculations, data cleaning, quick analysis and smaller business datasets.
  • SQL: Essential for retrieving, filtering, joining and aggregating data stored in relational databases.
  • Data visualisation: The ability to choose suitable charts and communicate patterns clearly.
  • Statistics: Helps analysts reason about distributions, comparisons, variation and relationships in data.
  • Business understanding: Analysts need to understand the context behind metrics and decisions.
  • Analytical thinking: Breaking an unclear problem into measurable questions is a core part of the role.
  • Communication: Findings need to be explained to people who may not work with data every day.
  • Python: Useful for repeatable analysis, data manipulation, automation and larger or more complex workflows.

Excel vs SQL vs Power BI vs Python

These tools are complementary rather than direct substitutes. The right tool depends on the data, the business question and the required output.

ToolCommon use
ExcelQuick calculations, spreadsheets, cleaning, formulas, pivot tables and exploratory analysis.
SQLQuerying databases, filtering records, joining tables and aggregating business data.
Power BIInteractive reports, dashboards, visual analysis and sharing business insights.
PythonData preparation, repeatable analysis, automation and advanced analytical workflows.

Microsoft's current Power BI Data Analyst guidance groups analyst work around preparing data, modelling data, visualising and analysing data, and managing or securing Power BI. This reflects why technical skills need to be combined with business understanding rather than learned in isolation.

How Data Analysts Work With Business Teams

Data analysts commonly work with teams such as finance, sales, marketing, operations, product and customer service. A stakeholder may describe a business problem in non-technical terms, while the analyst turns that requirement into metrics and analytical questions.

For example, a sales manager might ask why monthly revenue declined. An analyst could examine revenue by product, region, customer segment and time period, validate the underlying data, identify the strongest contributing patterns and present the findings in a dashboard or report.

Data Analyst vs Data Scientist vs BI Analyst

RoleTypical focus
Data AnalystAnalysing business data, reporting, dashboards, trends, performance metrics and decision support.
Data ScientistMore advanced statistical modelling, machine learning, experimentation and predictive or prescriptive analysis.
BI AnalystBusiness intelligence reporting, dashboards, data models and turning organisational data into accessible business information.

There is overlap between these roles, especially in modern analytics teams. Job titles and responsibilities can differ substantially between organisations, so the actual job description matters more than the title alone.

How AI Is Changing Data Analytics

AI is increasingly becoming an assistant within analytics workflows. For example, Microsoft documents capabilities in Power BI that can help users ask questions in natural language, create or explain visuals, and assist with calculations and report creation.

However, AI assistance does not remove the need for analytical judgement. Analysts still need to validate data quality, define the right business question, check whether an output is supported by the data and communicate limitations. Reliable analytics also depends on well-prepared data and clear business context.

What a Job-Ready Data Analytics Portfolio Should Show

A portfolio is stronger when it demonstrates the complete analytical process rather than only screenshots of dashboards. A useful project can show:

  • The business problem and project objective
  • The dataset and assumptions
  • Data cleaning and preparation steps
  • SQL queries or analytical calculations
  • Key metrics and analysis
  • A clear dashboard or visualisation
  • Important findings and their business meaning
  • Limitations and possible next questions

Example: An End-to-End Data Analytics Project

Imagine an e-commerce business wants to understand declining repeat purchases. A beginner-friendly project could start by defining repeat-purchase rate and customer segments. The analyst could clean customer and order data, use SQL to combine relevant tables, analyse purchase behaviour by segment and month, and build a Power BI dashboard.

The final output should not simply say that repeat purchases declined. It should show where the decline is concentrated, which patterns are supported by the data and which questions need further investigation.

Common Beginner Mistakes

  • Learning many tools without practising complete projects.
  • Building dashboards without first defining the business question.
  • Ignoring data cleaning and validation.
  • Focusing on visual design while missing the analytical story.
  • Using advanced techniques when a simple SQL query or spreadsheet analysis is enough.
  • Presenting correlations as proof of causation.
  • Copying project tutorials without understanding the decisions behind each step.

How to Build Data Analyst Skills Step by Step

  1. Start with Excel and basic data handling.
  2. Learn SQL thoroughly enough to work with real relational data.
  3. Build a foundation in statistics and analytical thinking.
  4. Learn a visualisation platform such as Power BI.
  5. Add Python for data analysis and automation.
  6. Build several end-to-end projects using realistic business questions.
  7. Document projects clearly and practise explaining findings.
  8. Prepare for interviews by solving SQL, analytical and business-case questions.

Frequently Asked Questions About Data Analysts

1. What does a data analyst do?

A data analyst prepares and analyses data to answer business questions, identify useful patterns, create reports or dashboards and communicate findings that support decisions.

2. What are the main skills required for a data analyst?

Common skills include Excel, SQL, data visualisation, statistics, analytical thinking, business understanding, communication and increasingly Python for more advanced workflows.

3. Is SQL important for data analysts?

Yes. SQL is widely used to retrieve, filter, join and aggregate data from relational databases, making it an important practical skill for many analyst roles.

4. Is Excel enough to become a data analyst?

Excel is an important foundation, but many analyst roles also require SQL, visualisation and other analytical skills. The required toolset depends on the employer and role.

5. Do data analysts need Python?

Not every analyst role requires Python, but it can be valuable for repeatable analysis, data manipulation, automation and workflows involving larger or more complex datasets.

6. What does a data analyst use Power BI for?

Power BI can be used to model data, build interactive reports and dashboards, visualise metrics and help stakeholders explore business information.

7. What is the difference between a data analyst and a data scientist?

Data analysts commonly focus on reporting, business analysis and decision support, while data scientists often work more deeply with statistical modelling, machine learning and predictive analysis.

8. Can AI replace data analysts?

AI can automate or assist with parts of analytics work, but analysts still need to define business questions, validate data and outputs, interpret results and communicate decisions responsibly.

9. What projects should a beginner data analyst build?

Good beginner projects use realistic business questions such as sales performance, customer behaviour, operations or marketing analysis and show data preparation, analysis, visualisation and conclusions.

10. How can I become job-ready as a data analyst?

Build practical skills in Excel, SQL, statistics and visualisation, add Python where useful, complete end-to-end projects and practise explaining your analytical decisions and findings.

Conclusion

A data analyst's job is fundamentally about turning data into useful evidence for business decisions. Tools matter, but the strongest foundation combines technical skills with problem definition, data quality, analytical reasoning and communication. Learning the workflow from business question to recommendation can help beginners build more relevant projects and prepare for real analyst responsibilities.
